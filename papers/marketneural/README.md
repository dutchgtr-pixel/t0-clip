# MarketNeural research manuscript

**Status: research manuscript draft, empirical comparison pending.** This is an independent thesis-style research document, not an academic degree or institutional submission.

Read the [complete manuscript](manuscript.md), which develops the problem, related work, temporal data protocol, neural architecture, Cox/tree comparison, leakage investigations, operations, historical results, limitations, and conclusions. The manuscript is approximately 8,000 words. The [BibTeX file](references.bib) contains verified primary references.

The manuscript distinguishes three evidence classes:

1. **Historical descriptive results:** earlier project reports and audited aggregate predictions, with cohort and provenance limitations.
2. **Public synthetic demonstration:** a runnable software exercise with artificial data and no claim of marketplace effectiveness.
3. **Controlled real-data comparison:** specified in the protocol, **not run**. No claim that neural models outperform Cox or trees is currently supported.

Supporting documents:

- [Research protocol](../../docs/research/PROTOCOL.md): hypotheses, splits, endpoints, budgets, analysis, and release gates.
- [Claim ledger](../../docs/research/CLAIM_LEDGER.md): what each statement is supported by and what it does not establish.
- [Model card](../../docs/research/MODEL_CARD.md): the compact benchmark, preserved architecture excerpt, and planned research model.
- [Data card](../../docs/research/DATA_CARD.md): synthetic generation, public input contract, temporal assumptions, and private-data exclusions.

Earlier reports elsewhere in `papers/` describe historical experiments. The new manuscript qualifies their stronger claims where the supporting evidence is narrower. Their original empirical definitions remain the context for the aggregate numbers cited here.

The manuscript does not contain private listing records, personal contact details, private infrastructure addresses, fitted private vocabularies, or local workstation paths. Public examples use artificial identifiers. Any editorial generalization of older source terminology does not convert their historical cohorts into a test of the new protocol.
